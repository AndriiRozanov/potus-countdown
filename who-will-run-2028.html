Who Will Run for President in 2028? Early Candidates & Predictions

Updated November 2026 · 7 min read · PresidencyClock.com

The 2028 presidential election is shaping up to be one of the most open races in decades. With Donald Trump constitutionally barred from a third term under the 22nd Amendment, both parties will field entirely new candidates for the first time since 2008 — and the jockeying is already well underway.

The picture sharpened after the 2026 midterms, in which Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate. Those results handed several Democratic governors and senators fresh evidence of their electability, while raising the stakes for a Republican field still defining itself in the Trump era. Here is what the early polling and the political landscape tell us heading into 2028.

The key rule: The 22nd Amendment bars Trump from running again. Any U.S. citizen who is at least 35 years old, a natural-born citizen, and has lived in the U.S. for 14+ years is eligible — with one exception: no one who has been elected president twice.

Where the early polling stands

It is still very early, and these numbers mostly reflect name recognition rather than likely primary results — no one has formally launched a 2028 campaign. With that caveat, here is a snapshot of national primary polling, based on Newsweek's analysis of RealClearPolling averages through November 2026:

Republicans: JD Vance ~39% · Marco Rubio ~26% · Donald Trump Jr. ~10% · Ron DeSantis ~8%

Democrats: Kamala Harris ~24% · Gavin Newsom ~14% · Pete Buttigieg ~13% · Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez ~12% · Jon Ossoff ~10% · Josh Shapiro ~6%

Republican candidates for 2028

The Republican primary will be the first fully open GOP contest since 2016. The central question is whether the party continues in the MAGA direction or pivots toward a more traditional conservative platform — and whether the next nominee runs as a continuation of Trump's politics, a modification of them, or a break.

JD Vance

Republican · Vice President

As Trump's vice president, Vance leads the early Republican polling and is the natural heir to the MAGA movement. Sitting vice presidents usually start with a built-in advantage. The caveat: the party's 2026 midterm losses could tie him to the administration's record, and he has acknowledged he may not ultimately run.

Marco Rubio

Republican · Secretary of State

Rubio sits second in early polling. His senior role in the administration keeps him prominent on foreign policy, which may insulate him somewhat from the midterm fallout. His challenge is converting international visibility into primary votes.

Donald Trump Jr.

Republican · Businessman

Carrying the family name, Trump Jr. registers in early polls despite holding no government office. His appeal rests on his connection to the MAGA base rather than a political record of his own.

Ron DeSantis

Republican · Governor of Florida

DeSantis ran in 2024 but withdrew before Iowa. A second attempt would require persuading Republican voters to reconsider a candidate they passed over last time. His pitch remains MAGA-aligned policy paired with a governing record.

Democratic candidates for 2028

On the Democratic side, the 2026 midterms reshaped the conversation. After winning both chambers of Congress, the party enters the 2028 cycle with momentum and a deep bench — governors and senators who can each point to recent wins. The early field is crowded and unsettled.

Kamala Harris

Democrat · Former Vice President

Harris leads early Democratic polling, largely on name recognition from her 2024 run. As the most familiar figure in the field, she starts ahead — but early polls measure recognition more than committed support, and she would face a crowded field of newer names.

Gavin Newsom

Democrat · Governor of California

Newsom has raised his national profile with travel to early-primary and Republican-leaning states. As governor of the largest state, he has a major platform; his challenge is broadening his appeal beyond the Democratic base.

Pete Buttigieg

Democrat · Former Secretary of Transportation

Buttigieg ran a competitive 2020 primary and served in the cabinet. At 46 in 2028, he offers a generational-change argument and has kept a steady national presence, including extensive campaigning in the 2026 midterms.

Jon Ossoff

Democrat · U.S. Senator (Georgia)

Ossoff won reelection in Georgia — a state Trump carried in 2024 — by a wide margin, giving him a strong electability case, and he leads some early prediction markets. Notably, he has said he has "zero interest" in a presidential run.

Josh Shapiro

Democrat · Governor of Pennsylvania

Shapiro won a second term in the pivotal swing state of Pennsylvania by a large margin. His ability to win decisively in a purple state makes him attractive to a party focused on electability.

Other names drawing attention include Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, who polls well with younger voters, and governors Andy Beshear of Kentucky and JB Pritzker of Illinois.

When will candidates officially announce?

Formal campaign announcements typically come 12–18 months before the general election — meaning 2027 for most serious contenders. Exploratory committees, fundraising, and early-state travel begin earlier, and much of that is already happening. The 2026 midterms were the first major test: candidates tied to strong November 2026 results enter 2027 with momentum.

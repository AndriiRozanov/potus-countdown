WHEN DOES THE NEW CONGRESS TAKE OFFICE?

Updated November 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

The new 120th Congress convenes at noon Eastern Time on January 3, 2027. The representatives and senators elected in the November 3, 2026 midterms are sworn in that day. Until then, the current 119th Congress keeps serving — a stretch known as the "lame-duck" period. The date is fixed by the 20th Amendment to the Constitution.

When the new Congress is sworn in

Although members of Congress are elected in early November, they do not take office right away. The newly elected Congress officially begins at noon on January 3 of the following year. For those elected in the 2026 midterms, that means January 3, 2027, when the 120th Congress convenes for the first time and its members take the oath of office.

This gap — about two months between election and swearing-in — is normal and built into the system. The winners spend that time preparing: hiring staff, setting up offices, and, for the party that won control, organizing committees and leadership.

Why January 3? The 20th Amendment

The start date is not arbitrary. It is set by the 20th Amendment to the U.S. Constitution, ratified in 1933, which fixes the beginning of congressional terms at noon on January 3. The same amendment sets the start of the presidential term at noon on January 20 — the reason Trump's term ends on January 20, 2029.

Before the 20th Amendment, newly elected members did not take their seats until March — four months after the election. That left a long "lame-duck" stretch in which defeated lawmakers still held power. The amendment shortened that gap to speed up the handover and make government more responsive to recent elections.

What happens on the first day

The opening day of a new Congress follows a set routine:

  • Members are sworn in. All 435 representatives and the newly elected senators take the oath of office.
  • The House elects a Speaker. Before any legislative work begins, the House votes for its Speaker — the chamber's leader and second in line to the presidency. This vote reflects which party won the majority.
  • Rules and organization. Each chamber adopts its rules and organizes committees, which the majority party controls.

The lame-duck period (November to January)

The weeks between the November election and the new Congress are called the lame-duck period. During this time the outgoing Congress is still fully in power and can pass laws — even members who lost their seats or are retiring keep their votes until January 3.

Lame-duck sessions are often used to finish urgent business: funding the government, passing a budget, or confirming nominations. Because some members no longer face re-election, these sessions can sometimes produce legislation that would be harder to pass at other times.

What the new Congress means for Trump

The makeup of the new Congress shapes the final two years of Trump's term. In the November 3, 2026 midterms, Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate (per The Associated Press, with some close races still being counted). That means that from January 3, 2027, Trump faces an opposition Congress: new legislation from the White House largely stalls, and congressional oversight and investigations increase. The new Congress does not, however, change the length of Trump's term — under the 20th Amendment, that ends at noon on January 20, 2029, no matter who controls Congress. For how the two chambers are elected, see our guide to how U.S. midterm elections work.

What about new governors?

Congress is federal and follows one national date, but governors are state offices, and each state sets its own inauguration day. Most newly elected governors are sworn in during January, but the exact date varies from state to state. Only the federal House and Senate follow the January 3 rule.

Frequently asked questions

When does the new Congress take office?

The 120th Congress convenes at noon ET on January 3, 2027. Members elected in the November 3, 2026 midterms are sworn in that day. Until then, the current 119th Congress continues.

Why does the new Congress start on January 3?

The 20th Amendment fixes the start of congressional terms at noon on January 3. Before it, members didn't take office until March, leaving a long post-election gap.

What is a lame-duck session?

The period after an election but before the new Congress convenes (roughly November to early January). The outgoing Congress keeps full power to pass laws during it.

When are newly elected members sworn in?

On the first day the new Congress convenes — January 3, 2027 for those elected in 2026. The House also elects its Speaker that day.

Does the new Congress affect Trump's term?

It affects how he governs, not how long. His term ends January 20, 2029 regardless. But the party controlling the new Congress from January 2027 shapes what legislation can pass in his final two years.

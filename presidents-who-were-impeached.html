US PRESIDENTS WHO WERE IMPEACHED

Updated August 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer

Three U.S. presidents have been impeached by the House of Representatives: Andrew Johnson (1868), Bill Clinton (1998), and Donald Trump — who was impeached twice (2019 and 2021). That is four impeachments in total. Crucially, none of them was ever removed from office: every one was acquitted by the Senate.

What does it mean to impeach a president?

Impeachment is the constitutional process for charging a sitting federal official — including the president — with serious misconduct, described in the Constitution as "treason, bribery, or other high crimes and misdemeanors." It is often misunderstood, so it helps to separate the two stages:

  • Impeachment (the House). The House of Representatives investigates and votes on "articles of impeachment." A simple majority is enough to impeach. This is essentially a formal accusation — like an indictment.
  • Trial and conviction (the Senate). The Senate then holds a trial. Removing the president requires a two-thirds supermajority to convict — a very high bar. Only conviction removes someone from office.

The most important point: impeached is not the same as removed

This is where most confusion comes from. Being impeached does not mean a president has been thrown out of office. It means the House formally charged them. The president stays in power unless the Senate convicts by a two-thirds vote. Every impeached president in U.S. history was acquitted by the Senate — so no president has ever actually been removed through impeachment.

The three presidents who were impeached

Andrew Johnson (1868)

The first president ever impeached. Johnson, who became president after Lincoln's assassination, clashed bitterly with Congress over how to rebuild the South after the Civil War. The House impeached him in 1868, largely over his removal of the Secretary of War in defiance of a law Congress had passed. The Senate fell one vote short of the two-thirds needed to convict, so he finished his term.

Bill Clinton (1998)

Clinton was impeached by the House in December 1998 on charges of perjury and obstruction of justice, stemming from an investigation into his personal conduct. The Senate acquitted him in February 1999, with neither charge coming close to the two-thirds threshold. He served out the rest of his second term.

Donald Trump (2019 and 2021)

Trump is the only president to be impeached twice, both times during his first term. The first impeachment, in December 2019, involved his dealings with Ukraine; the Senate acquitted him in February 2020. The second, in January 2021, came one week before his term ended and centered on the January 6 attack on the U.S. Capitol. The Senate acquitted him again in February 2021 — the vote was 57 to 43, short of the 67 required to convict.

What about Richard Nixon?

Many people assume Nixon was impeached, but he was not. During the Watergate scandal, the House Judiciary Committee approved articles of impeachment in 1974 — but before the full House could vote, Nixon resigned on August 9, 1974, becoming the only president to leave office that way. Because he resigned first, he was never formally impeached.

Why has no president ever been removed?

The answer is the two-thirds Senate threshold. Convicting a president requires 67 of 100 senators, which in practice means a large number of the president's own party must vote against them. That level of cross-party agreement has never happened in a presidential impeachment trial. The Founders deliberately set the bar high so that impeachment could not be used as a routine political weapon against a president by a bare majority.

Impeachment efforts during Trump's second term

Note: this section covers an ongoing, fast-moving political situation and reflects the status as of August 2026. It is likely to change.

Since President Trump began his second term in January 2025, several individual members of the House of Representatives have introduced articles of impeachment against him, and dozens of lawmakers have publicly called for impeachment or for use of the 25th Amendment. Much of this activity followed foreign-policy and military decisions in 2025 and 2026; the resolutions allege, among other things, that the president used military force abroad without congressional authorization.

As of August 2026, however, none of these resolutions has been adopted by the full House, which is the step required for an actual impeachment. Because impeachment begins with a majority vote in the House, whether any of these efforts advance depends heavily on which party controls the chamber — a question the November 2026 midterm elections will help decide. In short, filings and public calls are not the same as an impeachment: unless the House votes to adopt articles, no third impeachment has taken place.

Impeachment vs. the 25th Amendment

These two are often mentioned together but work very differently. Impeachment is for misconduct and runs through Congress. The 25th Amendment is about a president's inability to do the job (for example, due to illness) and is triggered by the vice president and Cabinet, not by Congress in the same way. Neither has ever been used to permanently remove a sitting president.

Frequently asked questions

How many U.S. presidents have been impeached?

Three: Andrew Johnson (1868), Bill Clinton (1998), and Donald Trump (twice, 2019 and 2021) — four impeachments in total. None were removed from office.

Has any president ever been removed by impeachment?

No. Impeachment by the House is only the first step; removal requires a two-thirds vote to convict in the Senate, and every impeached president has been acquitted.

Was Richard Nixon impeached?

No. He resigned in August 1974 before the full House could vote, though the House Judiciary Committee had approved articles of impeachment. He was never formally impeached.

How many times was Donald Trump impeached?

Twice, both during his first term (2019 and 2021) — the only president impeached twice. The Senate acquitted him both times.

Can an impeached president run for office again?

Yes, unless the Senate convicts and then separately votes to disqualify them. Because every impeached president was acquitted, none were barred — and Donald Trump went on to win the 2024 election.

Is Trump being impeached again in 2026?

As of August 2026, individual House members have introduced articles of impeachment and many have called for it, but the full House has not voted to adopt any. Filings and calls are not the same as an impeachment, so no third impeachment has occurred.
